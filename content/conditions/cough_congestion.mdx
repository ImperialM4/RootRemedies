## Introduction

Coughing, congestion, and feeling like you're stuffed up are some of the most common symptoms that happen when someone is sick. While many of these symptoms tend to go away on their own after a while, coughing, stuffiness, and congestion can make life much harder if you have a cold or upper respiratory infection.

For newcomers and returning readers of RootRemedies, if there's one thing that I hope you'd understand from us, it's that besides giving you a list of traditional remedies, we try to explain why these treatments work. For every remedy, whenever possible, I've done my best to include information about what modern science says regarding helping or supporting our understanding of these remedies.

These remedies are intended to provide temporary comfort and are **not substitutes for professional medical care**. If your symptoms worsen, last for several weeks, or are accompanied by serious symptoms, consult a healthcare professional.

::section-header{title="1. Warm Honey Water" subtitle="A simple remedy combining the soothing properties of honey with the comfort of warm fluids."}

::recipe{slug="warm-honey-water" index="0"}

### Why warm honey water may help

Honey has been studied extensively as a natural remedy for coughs. Its thick texture helps ease friction in throat tissues, allowing for reduced coughing. Honey also has mild antimicrobial properties due to its naturally high sugar concentration, acidic pH, and production of small amounts of **hydrogen peroxide** through the enzyme **glucose oxidase**.

Pairing honey with warm fluids can further improve comfort by helping keep the throat moisturized and allowing mucus to loosen.

A traditional remedy supported by research exploring honey's ability to soothe cough symptoms.
:::

::section-header{title="2. Ginger and Honey" subtitle="Combining two traditional ingredients with anti-inflammatory and soothing properties."}

::recipe{slug="ginger-honey" index="1"}

### Why ginger and honey may help

Ginger contains naturally occurring compounds that have been studied for their anti-inflammatory properties. Researchers have investigated ginger's ability to reduce inflammation in respiratory passages, including studies examining its effects on lung inflammation.

Honey added to the mixture assists by reducing irritation in the throat and helping soothe cough symptoms. Although more research is needed on this exact combination, both ingredients have biological properties that help explain their traditional use.

:::quote{attribution=""}
A traditional combination where ginger's anti-inflammatory potential pairs with honey's soothing effects.
:::

::divider

::section-header{title="3. Ghee, Cardamom, and Honey" subtitle="A traditional mixture focused on coating and soothing irritated throat tissues."}

::recipe{slug="ghee-cardamom-honey" index="2"}

### Why ghee, cardamom, and honey may help

Ghee's smooth, fatty texture allows it to coat irritated tissues in the throat as you swallow, which may make coughing feel less uncomfortable for a short period of time.

While scientists haven't found strong evidence that ghee directly treats coughs, cardamom contains naturally occurring compounds that have shown antioxidant and antimicrobial activity in laboratory studies. Combined with honey, this mixture may provide temporary relief from coughing discomfort.

:::quote{attribution=""}
A traditional remedy with soothing properties, although more research is needed to understand its direct effects.
:::

::divider

::section-header{title="4. Warm Onion Juice" subtitle="An unusual traditional remedy containing compounds studied for their biological properties."}

::recipe{slug="warm-onion-juice" index="3"}

### Why warm onion juice may help

It may seem unusual to drink warm onion juice, but onions contain many naturally occurring compounds that scientists have spent years studying.

One of these, **quercetin**, has been investigated for its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Other sulfur-containing compounds found in onions may also play a role in the body's immune response.

Researchers are still learning more about the degree to which onion juice can aid cough and congestion.

:::quote{attribution=""}
A traditional remedy with interesting biological compounds that continues to be explored by researchers.
:::

::section-header{title="5. Ginger and Lemon" subtitle="A traditional pairing that combines two ingredients with unique biological properties."}

::recipe{slug="ginger-lemon" index="4"}

### Why ginger and lemon may help

Ginger and lemon have often been paired together in traditional remedies, and some of their biological properties can help explain why.

Ginger contains naturally occurring compounds with anti-inflammatory properties, while lemon's acidity helps stimulate saliva production. Although this exact combination hasn't been studied extensively, the remedy may still contribute to temporary symptom relief.

:::quote{attribution=""}
A traditional combination where each ingredient contributes different properties that may improve comfort.
:::

::divider

::section-header{title="6. Pieces of Dates" subtitle="A nutritious traditional food that may support recovery during illness."}

::recipe{slug="dates" index="5"}

### Why dates may help

Dates are rich in carbohydrates, fiber, and potassium, which provide a quick source of energy when your appetite is reduced during illness.

While there is little evidence that dates directly relieve coughs or congestion, they serve as a nutritious, easily consumable snack that can help maintain nutrition during recovery.

:::quote{attribution=""}
A remedy focused less on direct symptom relief and more on supporting the body's recovery process.
:::

::section-header{title="7. Roasted Chickpeas and Warm Milk" subtitle="A traditional combination focused on nutrition and comfort during recovery."}

::recipe{slug="roasted-chickpeas-warm-milk" index="6"}

### Why roasted chickpeas and warm milk may help

Similar to the previous remedy, this one also focuses on helping your body recover.

Roasted chickpeas contain protein, fiber, and nutrients that support overall nutrition, while warm milk can help soothe throat irritation. Although scientists haven't found strong evidence that this combination clears congestion, it still serves as a good way to boost nutrition and recovery.

## Taking Care and Feeling Better

When your immune system clears the underlying infection, cough and congestion symptoms begin to lessen. As always, it is important to stay rested, hydrated, and let your body do its job to recover, even if you use any of these remedies.

If your symptoms worsen or persist for several weeks, talk to a healthcare professional to determine whether additional treatment is needed.
